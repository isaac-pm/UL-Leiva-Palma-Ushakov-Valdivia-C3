2 - How does the financial health of the residents change over the period covered by the dataset? How do wages compare to the overall cost of living in Engagement? Are there groups that appear to exhibit similar patterns? Describe your rationale for your answers. Limit your response to 10 images and 500 words.

Residents' financial health improved significantly over the 15-month period (March 2022 – May 2023). The citywide median ending balance rose from $2,810 to $33,637, indicating a substantial accumulation of financial cushion. Wages consistently covered the cost of living; median income remained well above median expenses in every month, with the median income-to-expenses ratio ranging from 2.31 to 2.71. Financial stress remained very low, with the citywide stress share (residents with expenses > wages or negative balance) peaking at only 1.1% in April 2022 and ending at 0.3% in May 2023. Education cohorts exhibit similar upward trends in balance but diverge in resilience. By May 2023, Graduates and Bachelors maintained the strongest positions (median balances of $55,827 and $45,083, respectively, with 0% stress). The 'Low education' cohort, while also improving, remained the most vulnerable with the lowest median balance ($18,135) and the highest stress share (1.8% in the final month, peaking at 7.1% in June 2022). Across all groups, housing was the dominant expense, consistently comprising over 50% of measured costs for most cohorts.
